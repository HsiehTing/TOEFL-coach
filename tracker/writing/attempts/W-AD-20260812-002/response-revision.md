How governments deal with people who lost their jobs due to technology advancement is something worth discussing. I believe that providing free job training opportunities should be implemented to efficiently resolve this issue.

As Andrew suggests, modern training programs are usually costly, so many people could not afford them without the financial help from the government. I also think that people whose jobs have been replaced by modern technologies would be less likely to seek opportunities to learn new technologies. For example, a cashier whose job was replaced by an automated checkout system can take a digital inventory management course and then apply for entry-level logistics jobs. In this way, a free modern skill training program provided by the government is a great incentive for them to step outside their comfort zone.

Some people suggest that giving people who lost their jobs financial aid is a better way, so that the money can directly help with their daily lives, allowing them to make better decisions. I agree that financial aid can greatly improve their daily lives. However, I don't think that the government should only give them money as a way to help them find job opportunities. Giving them money does not directly guide them toward better job opportunities, whereas  providing training programs does. And providing cash aid does not give them an incentive to learn new technologies. Overall, I think that a better solution is to provide free training in modern technologies.
