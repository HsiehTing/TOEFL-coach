# High-scoring model

Which policy governments should use to reduce carbon dioxide emissions from vehicles is an important question. I believe that expanding public transportation is the more effective solution.

As Claire suggests, if governments make public transportation more accessible, cheaper, and easier to use, people will drive less, further reducing air pollution. When buses and trains are more convenient and affordable than driving, commuters have less incentive to use private cars. Because one well-used bus or train can serve many commuters each day, public investment can reduce emissions for more people than a subsidy for individual electric-car purchases.

Although electric cars are useful in rural areas, cities have denser populations, so an expanded bus or rail system can replace many individual car trips at once. Governments should therefore prioritize public transportation in urban areas while continuing to support practical electric-car options for rural residents.
