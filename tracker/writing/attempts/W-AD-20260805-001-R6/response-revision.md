Which measures governments should take to control the emissions of carbon dioxide by vehicles is topic that worth discussing. I believe that expand public transportation is a more viable method to resolve this problem.

As Claire suggests, if governments make public transportation more accessible, cheaper and easier to use, people would drive less, further reducing air pollution. When buses and railways are way more convenient and cheaper than driving, commuters have less incentive to use private cars. Because one well-used bus or train can serve many commuters each day, public investment educes emissions for more people than a subsidy for an individual electric car purchase.

Although electric-cars are useful in rural areas, cities have denser populations, so an expanded train or rail system can replace many individual car trips at once. Governments should therefore prioritize public transportation in urban areas while continuing to support practical electric-car options for rural residents.
