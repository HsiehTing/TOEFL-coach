How governments deal with people who lost their jobs due to technology advancement is something worth discussing. I believe that providing free job training opportunities should be implemented to efficiently resolve this issue.

As Andrew suggests, modern training programs are usually costly, many people could not afford them without the financial help from the government. I also think that people who worked at a job that is replaced by modern technologies, would be less likely to seek opportunities to learn new technologies. For example, a cashier replaced by automation work flow can participate in digital inventory arrangement course, and further apply for logistics jobs. In this way, a free modern skill training program provided by the government is a great incentive for them to step outside their comfort zone.

Some people suggest that giving people who lost their jobs financial aid is the better way, so that the money can directly help their daily life, allowing them to make better decisions. I agree that financial aid can greatly improve their daily lives. However, I don't think that government should only give them money as a way to help them find job opportunities. Because bringing them money does not directly guide them for a better job opportunity comparing to providing training programs. And funding them does not bring the incentive of learning new technologies. Overall, I think that a better way is providing free modern technologies training programs.
